# Trace Commons 0.11.0: Private AI, a place to see your tools' model calls

*2026-09-07*

If you contribute coding sessions to Trace Commons, the app now has a
top-level destination called **Private AI** — on macOS, Windows and
Linux. Your AI calls go out through this computer, so you can see them
and choose who answers: the app answers your coding tools' model calls
from your own machine, keeps the record there, and passes each call on
to whoever you have set up to answer it.

Think of it as a VPN for AI, with the same honesty a VPN deserves: your
calls go out through a point you control, which sees them and keeps the
record. It does not make the call invisible to whoever answers it — each
call is still passed on to the provider you configured. What changes is
that *you* now have the record, and the say over who answers.

## Where the name comes from

One of the destinations registered by default is NEAR AI's
[Private Inference](https://docs.near.ai/cloud/private-inference), which
runs models inside hardware trusted execution environments — Intel TDX
for the virtual machine, NVIDIA's GPU TEE for the model and its
computation, with the TLS connection terminated inside the enclave
rather than at a load balancer. In their words: "It ensures that AI
computations happen in a completely isolated environment where no
one—not the cloud provider, not the model provider, not even NEAR AI can
access your data," verifiable "through cryptographic attestation." That
is their claim, about their hardware, and their page explains how to
check it yourself.

It belongs to the destination, not to this screen. A call answered by
NEAR AI gets those properties; a call your tool sends to Anthropic or
OpenAI directly gets whatever those services provide. The screen does
not add confidentiality to anything — it is where you see your calls
and choose a destination that has it.

One more line to keep straight, because it is easy to conflate: a
session you choose to contribute to Trace Commons is shared with Trace
Commons. That is the product, and it is your decision per session. TEE
confidentiality is about who can read a call while it is being answered;
it says nothing about what you later choose to share.

## What is on the screen

**A list of the coding tools on your computer** — Claude Code and Codex
today — each showing one of three states: not connected, connected but
nothing has arrived yet, or answering. That last distinction is the whole
point. A settings file with the right value in it is not evidence that a
single call was ever answered; before this release you could turn the
feature on and have no way to tell whether anything was actually using
it. Now the screen tells you when a call from a tool genuinely reached
your computer and was answered there.

**Connecting a tool is one decision at a time, behind a preview.** Before
anything is written, the app shows you the exact change it would make to
that tool's own settings file, and writes nothing until you confirm. If
you already filled in a setting yourself, it is reported and left exactly
as you had it — never overwritten.

**The switch is in the tray (menu bar on macOS) and on a keyboard
shortcut.** Turning it off acts immediately — that only reduces what your
computer will answer. Turning it on opens the Private AI screen first, on
purpose: while it is on, anything else running on your computer can send
calls through it, charged to the accounts you have set up. That is a
decision to make with the consequence in front of you, not from a menu.

## The limits, plainly

- **Two tools, not four.** Gemini is not supported: the mechanism can
  write one of two API shapes, and neither is Gemini's. Cline keeps no
  settings file we can find. We would rather say this than imply more is
  coming.
- The feature does nothing until you connect a tool. Turning the switch
  on, by itself, changes where no tool sends its calls.
- Despite the name, the screen itself promises no privacy. Whatever
  confidentiality your calls have comes from where they are answered,
  not from this feature: choose a destination without it and there is
  none. The screen moves where your calls are answered from, keeps the
  record on your computer, and shows you the choice — the destination
  still sees what you send it.

## Along the way

Building this on IronWire, the local component that does the answering,
we found and fixed seven defects in it — all merged upstream
([#33](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/33),
[#34](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/34),
[#37](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/37),
[#39](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/39),
[#40](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/40),
[#42](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/42),
[#45](https://github.com/nearai/ironwire/issues/45)). Two worth naming:
connecting a tool could move a setting you had already chosen, and a
preview of additions could commit a removal. Those fixes reach you
whether or not you use this screen.

## Getting it

The desktop app updates itself. The CLI is on Homebrew.
